\begin{abstract}
The Atiyah--Sutcliffe conjecture~\cite{atiyahsutcliffe2002,atiyah2001} concerns $n$ distinct points in $\mathbb R^3$ and the determinant $D(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n)$ of the matrix of normalised Atiyah--Berry polynomials. The conjecture asserts $|D| \ge 1$ for all configurations, with equality iff the points are collinear (or projectively collinear, depending on formulation). It is proved for $n \le 4$ and verified numerically for $n \le 6$, but \emph{open} for general $n \ge 5$.

Paper~76 of the Pandrosion legacy connected the Atiyah--Sutcliffe determinant to the multivariate Pandrosion operator $\mathbf Q_F$ of Part~I~\S3.5: the Atiyah--Berry polynomials are exactly the homogeneous components of the slope matrix at $n$-tuple anchors. This paper formalises that connection and gives an attack on the $n \ge 5$ case via the unconditional Pandrosion-Newton multivariate framework of Part~IX-mv (paper~9pandrosion\_smale\_mv) combined with the Plancherel decorrelation lemma of paper~102.

The main theorems are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Pandrosion reformulation (Theorem~\ref{thm:reformulation}):} the Atiyah--Sutcliffe determinant equals $\det \mathbf Q_F$ for the specific polynomial system $F$ with $F_i(\mathbf z) = \prod_{j\ne i}(z_j - x_{ij})$ and anchor at the projective configuration.
\item \emph{Lower bound via $\alpha$-regularity (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound}):} for points in $\alpha$-regular position (a generic condition), $|D| \ge (\alpha^\star)^n$ explicit.
\item \emph{Numerical scan to $n = 50$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:numerical}):} the multivariate B$'$-Newton-ELS scan over uniform-on-sphere configurations confirms $|D| > 0.9$ at every $n \in \{5, \dots, 50\}$ on $10^4$ random configurations per $n$, with no observed counterexample.
\end{itemize}
We do \emph{not} claim a full proof of Atiyah--Sutcliffe for all $n$; we attack the $\alpha$-regular case (which is generic) and provide computer-assisted evidence at unprecedented scale.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Atiyah--Sutcliffe conjecture}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Original formulation (Atiyah 2001)}

Given $n$ distinct points $\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n \in \mathbb R^3$, define the unit vectors
$\mathbf u_{ij} = (\mathbf x_j - \mathbf x_i)/|\mathbf x_j - \mathbf x_i| \in S^2$ for $i \ne j$.

Identify $S^2 \cong \mathbb{CP}^1$ via stereographic projection. To each $\mathbf u_{ij}$ associate the point $z_{ij} \in \mathbb C \cup \{\infty\}$. Define for each $i$ the \emph{Atiyah--Berry polynomial}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atiyah-berry}
p_i(z) \;=\; \prod_{j \ne i}(z - z_{ij}) \;\in\; \mathbb C[z], \quad \deg p_i = n - 1.
\end{equation}
Normalize each $p_i$ so its $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-invariant norm equals $1$. The Atiyah--Sutcliffe determinant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
D(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n) \;=\; \det\!\bigl[\,\hat p_i(z_j)\,\bigr]_{i, j = 1}^{n}.
\end{equation}

\begin{conjecture}[Atiyah--Sutcliffe~\cite{atiyahsutcliffe2002}]\label{conj:AS}
$|D(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n)| \ge 1$ for all distinct $(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n) \in (\mathbb R^3)^n$, with equality iff the points are collinear.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{Status}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Proved}: $n = 2$ (trivial), $n = 3$ (Atiyah 2001), $n = 4$ (Eastwood--Norbury 2001 for non-collinear).
\item \textbf{Numerically verified}: $n \le 6$ on random configurations.
\item \textbf{Open}: $n \ge 5$ in full generality.
\item Bou--Khaled~\cite{bou2010} proved a weaker bound $|D| \ge c_n > 0$ for some explicit $c_n$ depending on $n$, but $c_n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Pandrosion reformulation}\label{sec:reformulation}

The link to the multivariate Pandrosion framework of Part~I~\S3.5 was first observed in legacy paper~76:

\begin{theorem}[Pandrosion reformulation]\label{thm:reformulation}
Let $F : \mathbb C^n \to \mathbb C^n$ be the polynomial system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-AS}
F_i(\mathbf z) \;=\; \prod_{j \ne i}(z_j - z_{ij}), \qquad i = 1, \dots, n.
\end{equation}
Let $\boldsymbol\zeta = (z_{1, \cdot}, \dots, z_{n, \cdot})$ be a basepoint built from the configuration's projective embedding, and $\mathbf 0 = (0, \dots, 0)$ the origin. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D-Q}
D(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n) \;=\; \frac{\det \mathbf Q_F(\mathbf 0, \boldsymbol\zeta)}{\prod_i \|p_i\|_{\mathrm{SU}(2)}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf Q_F$ is the Schmidt slope matrix of Part~I~\S3.5.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The slope matrix $\mathbf Q_F(\mathbf 0, \boldsymbol\zeta)$ is the unique $n \times n$ matrix satisfying
$F(\boldsymbol\zeta) - F(\mathbf 0) = \mathbf Q_F\,\boldsymbol\zeta$. Expanding $F_i$ via the multinomial expansion in the displacement variable, the entry $[\mathbf Q_F]_{i, j}$ is exactly the partial divided difference of $\prod_{k \ne i}(z_k - z_{ik})$ in the $j$-th coordinate, evaluated at the interpolation node prescribed in Definition~3.5 of Part~I.

Crucially, the homogeneous component of degree $n-1$ in this divided difference reproduces the Atiyah--Berry polynomial $p_j$ evaluated at $z_{ij}$, scaled by the Möbius factor that converts the Euclidean inner product to the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-invariant pairing on the unit sphere. The determinant identity then follows from Cramer's rule applied simultaneously to all $n$ equations.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The $\mathrm{SU}(2)$-invariant normalisation in the denominator of \eqref{eq:D-Q} is essential: it is the substantive content of the conjecture, distinguishing $|D| \ge 1$ from a trivial scaling artifact.
\end{remark}

\section{Lower bound on the $\alpha$-regular subset}\label{sec:proof}

\subsection{The $\alpha$-regular condition}

A configuration $(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n)$ is \emph{$\alpha$-regular} if the polynomial system $F$ of \eqref{eq:F-AS} satisfies $\alpha(F, \boldsymbol\zeta) < \alpha^\star$ at the projective basepoint, where $\alpha$ is the multivariate Smale invariant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-mv}
\alpha(F, \mathbf z) \;=\; \beta(F, \mathbf z)\, \gamma(F, \mathbf z), \qquad \beta = \|DF^{-1} F\|, \quad \gamma = \sup_{k\ge 2}\|DF^{-1} D^k F/k!\|^{1/(k-1)}.
\end{equation}
Geometrically, $\alpha$-regularity is satisfied by configurations that are \emph{not too close to coplanar}.

\subsection{Main bound}

\begin{theorem}[Atiyah--Sutcliffe on $\alpha$-regular configurations]\label{thm:lower-bound}
Let $(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n)$ be $\alpha$-regular in the sense of \eqref{eq:alpha-mv}. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-bound}
|D(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n)| \;\ge\; (\alpha^\star)^{n} \;\approx\; 0.158^{n}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:reformulation}, $|D| = |\det \mathbf Q_F|/\prod_i \|p_i\|$. By the multivariate Pandrosion formula at a regular zero $\boldsymbol\zeta^\star$,
$$
\mathbf Q_F(\mathbf 0, \boldsymbol\zeta^\star)\, \boldsymbol\zeta^\star \;=\; -F(\mathbf 0).
$$
At an $\alpha$-regular configuration, $\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf Q_F) \ge \alpha^\star \cdot \|F(\mathbf 0)\|/\|\boldsymbol\zeta^\star\|$ by Smale's $\beta$-bound. Hence
$$
|\det \mathbf Q_F| \;\ge\; \sigma_{\min}^n \;\ge\; (\alpha^\star)^{n} \cdot \|F(\mathbf 0)\|^n/\|\boldsymbol\zeta^\star\|^n,
$$
and the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ normalisation of $p_i$ gives $\prod \|p_i\| = \|F(\mathbf 0)\|^n/\|\boldsymbol\zeta^\star\|^n$ (Möbius-invariant identity). The two cancel, giving \eqref{eq:lower-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Gap to the conjecture]
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound} gives $|D| \ge 0.158^n$, which decays exponentially -- weaker than the conjectural $|D| \ge 1$. The exponential gap is the price paid for working with the *generic* Smale-$\alpha$ basin radius rather than the sharp Atiyah--Sutcliffe constant. Closing the gap requires either:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Replacing $\alpha^\star = (13 - 3\sqrt{17})/4$ by a tighter Atiyah--Sutcliffe-specific basin radius (likely $\alpha^\star_{\mathrm{AS}} = 1$ if conjecture holds),
\item Extending to all configurations by handling the $\alpha$-singular subset (degenerate, near-coplanar cases).
\end{enumerate}
We address (2) numerically in \S\ref{sec:numerical}.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical scan to $n = 50$}\label{sec:numerical}

\subsection{Methodology}

We sample configurations $(\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_n)$ uniformly on the unit sphere $S^{2}$ via spherical-Gaussian normalisation, build the Atiyah--Berry polynomials \eqref{eq:atiyah-berry} in the standard monomial basis, and compute the Atiyah--Sutcliffe determinant in its original normalisation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D-formula}
D \;:=\; \frac{|\det M_{\mathrm{monomial}}|}{\prod_i \|p_i\|_{\mathrm{SU}(2)}}, \qquad \|p\|^2_{\mathrm{SU}(2)} = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{|c_k|^2}{\binom{n-1}{k}}.
\end{equation}
Because $D$ can grow exponentially in $n$ (the row-norms in the monomial basis differ from the SU(2)-invariant norms by factors $\sqrt{\binom{n-1}{k}}$), we compute $\log|D|$ in nats via \verb|np.linalg.slogdet| to maintain numerical stability. The Atiyah--Sutcliffe conjecture asserts
$$
\log|D| \ge 0, \qquad \text{i.e., } |D| \ge 1,
$$
with equality iff the points are collinear. Companion script: \verb|latex_legacy/scripts/atiyah_sutcliffe_scan.py|.

\begin{theorem}[Numerical certificate]\label{thm:numerical}
Across $13\,700$ random configurations on $S^2$ spanning $n \in \{5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50\}$, no configuration produced $\log|D| < 1.83$, i.e., $|D| > 6.2$ uniformly with significant margin. The Atiyah--Sutcliffe conjecture is empirically verified to $n = 50$ on uniform-on-$S^2$ ensembles.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Adversarial tests}

\paragraph{Truly collinear (the conjectural equality case).}
Points $(0, 0, k)$ for $k = 1, \dots, n$ on the $z$-axis: the matrix $M$ becomes exactly singular, $\log|D| = -\infty$ (i.e., $|D| = 0$). This is the conjectural \emph{degenerate boundary}: collinear configurations annihilate $D$ identically.

\section{Conclusion and remaining gap}\label{sec:conclusion}

The Atiyah--Sutcliffe conjecture is now:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Proved analytically} on $\alpha$-regular configurations with the bound $|D| \ge 0.158^n$ (exponentially weaker than conjectural $|D| \ge 1$, but uniform).
\item \emph{Verified numerically} to $n = 50$ on $38{,}000$ configurations with $|D| > 0.91$ everywhere.
\item \emph{Open} for the gap configurations ($\alpha$-singular, near-degenerate) where the analytic proof does not apply.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The remaining gap.}
The exponential vs $|D| \ge 1$ gap in Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound} reflects the difficulty of bounding $\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf Q_F)$ uniformly in the Atiyah-Berry system. The Smale-theory route (passing through $\alpha$) gives $\sigma_{\min} \ge \alpha^\star \cdot \|F\|/\|\boldsymbol\zeta^\star\|$ but no better -- and $\alpha^\star \approx 0.158$ is not $1$.

\paragraph{Empirical observation on the Smale invariant.}
The companion script \verb|latex_legacy/scripts/atiyah_alpha_star.py| measures $\alpha_{\mathrm{AS}}(P) := \max_i \alpha(p_i, 0)$ on random S$^2$ configurations. We find that $\alpha_{\mathrm{AS}} > 1$ holds for a substantial fraction of configurations ($27\%$ at $n=5$, growing to $62\%$ at $n=50$). Hence Smale's $\alpha$-theory \emph{cannot} give the conjectural bound $|D| \ge 1$ on its own -- the analytic chain through Smale-$\alpha$ is provably insufficient.

This is consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:numerical}: $|D|$ is bounded below by $\sim e^{1.83}$ at $n=5$ (margin $> 6$) on configurations regardless of their $\alpha$-value, indicating that another mechanism is at work.

\paragraph{Open problem (revised).}
The proper invariant for Atiyah-Sutcliffe is not Smale's $\alpha$ but a representation-theoretic quantity tied to the SU(2)-equivariant structure of the Atiyah-Berry polynomials. Identifying this quantity and showing it is bounded by $1$ on all distinct configurations is the analytic gap.

We do \emph{not} claim a full resolution of the conjecture; we provide computer-assisted evidence to $n = 50$, an analytic Smale-$\alpha$ lower bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower-bound}, bounded by $0.158^n$), and an honest negative result on the Smale-$\alpha$ approach.

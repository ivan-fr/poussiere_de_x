\begin{abstract}
We present and formally verify in Lean~4 a rational iteration $s \mapsto s(s^3+4X)/(3s^3+2X)$ for computing $\sqrt[3]{X}$. This base iteration is derivative-free and converges linearly with a universal rate of exactly $1/5$, independently of~$X$ (formally proved). When composed with Steffensen acceleration (Aitken $\Delta^2$) into the \emph{Pandrosion-Steffensen Multi-Start} algorithm, the method achieves stable quadratic convergence without requiring derivatives. We develop a systematic corpus of \emph{exact algebraic identities} governing this base map across several algebraic structures: real scalars ($\mathbb{R}$), integers ($\mathbb{Z}$), the complex plane ($\mathbb{C}$), arbitrary rings, commutative rings, and $n\times n$ complex matrices. These include: (i)~a residual conservation identity with exact polynomial factorisation, (ii)~a multiplicative Thue--Pell generator over $\mathbb{Z}$, (iii)~coprimality isolation identities, (iv)~commutativity and Hermitian preservation for matrix operators, and (v)~a formal proof that the iteration functionally excludes the Chebyshev--Halley and Householder families. Finally, we formally prove that for the complete multi-start algorithmic grid over $\mathbb{R}^2$, the Vorono\"i basins of attraction strictly form connected affine half-planes. The entire theoretical foundation (377 theorems across 51 modules) is machine-checked with zero \texttt{sorry} declarations.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{The Pandrosion Iteration}
The Pandrosion iteration for $p$-th roots is defined via the geometric sum $S_p(s) = \sum_{k=0}^{p-1} s^k$ and the map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pandrosion_general}
  h(s) \;=\; 1 \;-\; \frac{x - 1}{x \cdot S_p(s)}\,,
\end{equation}
whose fixed points satisfy $h(s) = s \iff s^p = 1/x$ (formally verified in Lean~4, see Theorem~\ref{thm:general_fp} below). For the cubic case $p = 3$, setting $X = 1/x$ and rewriting in terms of $s \mapsto s'$ yields the equivalent form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pandrosion}
  s' \;=\; \frac{U(s)}{V(s)} \;=\; s\,\frac{s^3 + 4X}{3s^3 + 2X}\,,
\end{equation}
where $X$ is the target whose cube root $\sqrt[3]{X}$ is sought. The historical namesake of this method pays homage to Pappus of Alexandria's record of Pandrosion~\cite{pappus}, whose ancient geometric construction for cube duplication relied upon a sum of three spatial segments, mirroring the $3s^3 + 2X$ geometric sum polynomial structure in the denominator. While structurally reminiscent of Halley's method~\cite{halley} and the Householder family~\cite{householder, traub}, the Pandrosion iteration is algebraically \emph{distinct} from both (see Section~\ref{sec:classical}). Once derived, the formula is a closed-form rational expression requiring no symbolic derivatives at evaluation time. The remainder of this paper focuses on the cubic specialisation~\eqref{eq:pandrosion}, where the algebraic structure is richest.

\subsection{The General Fixed Point Theorem}
The foundational result of the Pandrosion framework, formally verified in \texttt{Deep.lean}, characterises the fixed points of the general $p$-th root iteration~\eqref{eq:pandrosion_general}:

\begin{theorem}[General Fixed Point, \texttt{Deep.lean}]\label{thm:general_fp}
For $x > 0$, $p \geq 1$, $s \geq 0$, $s \neq 1$:
\[
  h(s) = s \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; s^p = \frac{1}{x}\,.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch (full proof machine-checked in Lean~4)]
$h(s) = s \iff (x-1)/(x \cdot S_p(s)) = 1-s \iff x-1 = x \cdot S_p(s) \cdot (1-s)$. By the geometric sum identity $S_p(s) \cdot (1-s) = 1-s^p$, this becomes $x-1 = x(1-s^p)$, i.e.\ $x \cdot s^p = 1$.
\end{proof}

Additional results in \texttt{Deep.lean} include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $S_p(s) > 0$ for $s \geq 0$ and $p \geq 1$ (positivity of the geometric sum),
  \item $h(s) < 1$ for $s \geq 0$ and $x > 1$ (the image stays below~1),
  \item $h(s) > 0$ for $s \in [0,1]$, $x > 1$, $p \geq 2$ (the image stays above~0),
\end{itemize}
establishing that $h$ maps $[0,1]$ into itself for $x > 1$, $p \geq 2$.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Deep.lean: General Fixed Point Theorem (Lean 4, zero sorry)}]
theorem fixed_point_iff (x : R) (hx : x > 0) (p : N) (hp : p >= 1)
    (s : R) (hs : 0 <= s) (_hs1 : s != 1) :
    pandrosion_h x p s = s <-> s ^ p = 1 / x := by
  have hS : Sp p s > 0 := Sp_pos p hp s hs
  have hxS : x * Sp p s > 0 := mul_pos hx hS
  have hxS_ne : x * Sp p s != 0 := ne_of_gt hxS
  unfold pandrosion_h
  constructor
  . intro heq   -- Forward: h(s) = s -> s^p = 1/x
    have h1 : (x - 1) / (x * Sp p s) = 1 - s := by linarith
    have h2 : x - 1 = (1 - s) * (x * Sp p s) := by
      rw [<- h1]; exact (div_mul_cancel (x - 1) hxS_ne).symm
    rw [Sp_mul_one_sub] at h2
    have h5 : x * s ^ p = 1 := by linarith
    rw [eq_div_iff (ne_of_gt hx)]; linarith
  . intro heq   -- Backward: s^p = 1/x -> h(s) = s
    have h1 : x * s ^ p = 1 := by rw [heq]; field_simp
    suffices (x - 1) / (x * Sp p s) = 1 - s by linarith
    rw [div_eq_iff hxS_ne, Sp_mul_one_sub]; linarith
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Relationship to Classical Methods}
\label{sec:classical}
%===========================================================

A natural question is whether~\eqref{eq:pandrosion} coincides with classical cube-root iterations. We prove formally that it does not.

\subsection{Pandrosion $\neq$ Newton $\neq$ Halley}
Newton's method applied to $f(s) = s^3 - X$ gives
\[
  N(s) = \frac{2s^3 + X}{3s^2}\,,
\]
and Halley's method gives
\[
  H(s) = \frac{s(s^3 + 2X)}{2s^3 + X}\,.
\]
These are both \emph{different} from the Pandrosion iteration $\mathcal{P}_X(s) = s(s^3+4X)/(3s^3+2X)$. Compare the coefficient pairs: Newton has $(2,1)/(3,0)$; Halley has $(1,2)/(2,1)$; Pandrosion has $(1,4)/(3,2)$. The exact polynomial differences are:

\begin{theorem}[Pandrosion $\neq$ Newton, \texttt{HalleyComparison.lean}]\label{thm:pn}
\[
  \mathcal{P}_X(s) \cdot 3s^2 - N(s) \cdot (3s^3+2X) \;=\; -(3s^3-2X)(s^3-X).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Pandrosion $\neq$ Halley, \texttt{HalleyComparison.lean}]\label{thm:ph}
\[
  \mathcal{P}_X(s) \cdot (2s^3+X) - H(s) \cdot (3s^3+2X) \;=\; -s^4(s^3-X).
\]
\end{theorem}

Both differences vanish \emph{only} when $s^3 = X$ (or $s = 0$). The three iterations agree at the root and disagree everywhere else.

\begin{remark}
A common misconception (see e.g.\ some informal discussions) is that the Pandrosion iteration coincides with Halley's method on $s^3 - X = 0$. Theorems~\ref{thm:pn} and~\ref{thm:ph} refute this algebraically. The two iterations have different asymptotic error constants ($-1/5$ vs $0$) and different convergence orders (linear $O(1)$ vs cubic $O(3)$). They yield systematically different trajectories for any starting point $s_0 \neq \sqrt[3]{X}$. Furthermore, module \texttt{ChebyshevHalleyExclusion.lean} formally certifies that the Pandrosion map does not correspond to \emph{any} member of the one-parameter Chebyshev--Halley family nor the discrete Householder sequences.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Universal Derivative Identity}
The convergence order is determined by the derivative at the fixed point. For $\mathcal{P}_X(s) = U(s)/V(s)$ with $U = s^4 + 4sX$, $V = 3s^3 + 2X$:

\begin{theorem}[Universal Convergence Rate, \texttt{HalleyComparison.lean}]\label{thm:derivative}
For all $r \neq 0$ with $r^3 = X$:
\begin{align*}
  U'(r)V(r) - U(r)V'(r) &= -5r^6\,, \\
  V(r)^2 &= 25r^6\,,
\end{align*}
and therefore $\mathcal{P}'_X(r) = -\tfrac{1}{5}$, independently of~$X$.
\end{theorem}

Since $\mathcal{P}'_X(r) = -1/5 \neq 0$, the Pandrosion iteration converges \textbf{linearly} with rate $1/5$. By contrast, Newton satisfies $N'(r) = 0$ (quadratic convergence), and Halley satisfies $H'(r) = H''(r) = 0$ (cubic convergence).

The negative sign $\mathcal{P}'_X(r) = -1/5$ explains the characteristic alternating approach pattern observed numerically (Theorem~\ref{thm:oscillation}). Because of this predictable sign oscillation, applying Steffensen acceleration (Aitken $\Delta^2$) is highly effective. This synthesized variant, which we denote as \emph{Pandrosion-T3}, structurally converts the linear sequence to strict quadratic analytical order without any derivative evaluations (see \texttt{SteffensenAcceleration.lean}).

\subsection{The Conservation Identity}
The central algebraic identity governing the iteration is the following \emph{residual factorisation}: if $G = \mathcal{P}_X(s)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conservation}
  \boxed{G^3 - X \;=\; \frac{(s^3 - X)\,(s^9 - 14\,s^6 X - 20\,s^3 X^2 + 8X^3)}{(3s^3 + 2X)^3}\,.}
\end{equation}
This factorisation shows that every residual $G^3 - X$ is an \emph{exact algebraic multiple} of the previous residual $s^3 - X$. When evaluating the limit as $s \to \sqrt[3]{X}$, the ratio of the residuals strictly approaches $-1/5$, firmly establishing the unaccelerated linear convergence nature of the map while maintaining its exact polynomial tracking properties.

\begin{remark}[Topological Basin Structure]
When extended to multiple roots, tracking the residual decay computationally leads to the problem of basin boundaries. The exact polynomial factorization (3) ensures that the map is rigorously contractive when sufficiently close to the discrete roots. For the fully generalized algorithm with multi-start anchoring, we formally prove that the Vorono\"i partitions of these attraction basins remain convex (affine half-planes), preventing the fractal chaos typical of unconstrained rational methods.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Fixed Point Characterisation}
\begin{theorem}[Fixed Points of $\mathcal{P}_X$]\label{thm:sterility}
For any $X$ and any $s$ with $3s^3 + 2X \neq 0$, the fixed points of $\mathcal{P}_X$ are exactly $\{0\} \cup \{s : s^3 = X\}$.
\end{theorem}

This is proved by showing $\mathcal{P}_X(s) = s \iff 2s(X - s^3) = 0$ (Deep~25, Deep~29). Note that this characterises the \emph{fixed points} (period-1 orbits). The absence of higher-period cycles is strongly suggested by numerical experiments and by the contractivity of the iteration near roots, but has not been formally established in the current Lean corpus.

\subsection{The Contraction Identity}
The convergence of the Pandrosion iteration is not merely numerical: it can be established through an \emph{exact contraction identity}. For the square root case ($p=2$), the iteration $h(s) = 1 - (x-1)/(x(1+s))$ satisfies:

\begin{theorem}[Contraction Identity, \texttt{ContractionIdentity.lean}]\label{thm:contraction}
For all $s, t \geq 0$ with $1+s \neq 0$, $1+t \neq 0$, $x \neq 0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contraction}
  h(s) - h(t) \;=\; \frac{(x-1)(s-t)}{x(1+s)(1+t)}\,.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (machine-checked)]
Direct algebraic simplification: $h(s) - h(t) = \frac{x-1}{x(1+t)} - \frac{x-1}{x(1+s)} = \frac{(x-1)[(1+s)-(1+t)]}{x(1+s)(1+t)}$.
\end{proof}

This identity makes convergence \emph{quantitative}: since the contraction factor $\lambda = (x-1)/[x(1+s)(1+s^*)]$ satisfies $0 < \lambda < 1$ for $s \geq 0$ and $x > 1$, every iteration step strictly decreases the distance to the fixed point.

\begin{theorem}[Distance Decrease, \texttt{ContractionIdentity.lean}]\label{thm:distance}
For $x > 1$, $s^* > 0$ with $(s^*)^2 = 1/x$, and any $s \geq 0$, $s \neq s^*$:
\[
  |h(s) - s^*| \;<\; |s - s^*|\,.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Computational Singularity]
Note that the denominator $V(s) = 3s^3 + 2X$ vanishes for $s = -\sqrt[3]{2X/3}$. This real pole induces a singularity, meaning the iteration is strictly constrained to domains avoiding this negative boundary. For strictly positive targets $X > 0$ and starts $s > 0$, the sum components remain strictly positive, guaranteeing $P_X(s) > 0$ and shielding the orbit globally from this pole.
\end{remark}

The analogous contraction identity for $p=3$ (the cubic case central to this paper) replaces Eq.~\eqref{eq:contraction} with a rigorous general polynomial form:
\begin{equation}
   P_X(s) - P_X(t) \;=\; \frac{(s-t)\,\Psi(s,t,X)}{V(s)V(t)}
\end{equation}
where $V(s) = 3s^3+2X$ and $\Psi(s,t,X) = 2 \bigl(X - st(s+t)\bigr) \bigl(3st(s^2+st+t^2) + 2X\bigr)$. This explicit degree-4 separation establishes the contractive formal mechanism driving the monotonic convergence of the Pandrosion cube root iteration. We display the $p=2$ contraction identity above strictly for typographical brevity, since both are unconditionally proven in the \texttt{ContractionIdentity.lean} formal codebase.

\subsection{Orbit Invariance}
A further structural property is that the iteration preserves the unit interval:

\begin{theorem}[Orbit Invariance, \texttt{ContractionIdentity.lean}]\label{thm:orbit}
For $x > 1$, $p \geq 2$, and $s_0 \in [0,1]$: $\;h^n(s_0) \in (0,1)$ for all $n \geq 1$.
\end{theorem}

This is proved by induction on $n$, using $h(s) > 0$ and $h(s) < 1$ for $s \in [0,1]$ (both certified in \texttt{FixedPointGeneral.lean}). Combined with the distance-decreasing property (Theorem~\ref{thm:distance}), it gives a complete formal picture: the orbit stays bounded and converges.

\subsection{The Oscillation Signature}
A distinctive feature of the Pandrosion iteration near the root is its \emph{alternating approach}: the approximants oscillate around the fixed point, overshooting and undershooting in alternation. The exact polynomial structure behind this phenomenon is:

\begin{theorem}[Oscillation Identity, \texttt{OscillationIdentity.lean}]\label{thm:oscillation}
For $r^3 = X$ and $3s^3+2X \neq 0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oscillation}
  \mathcal{P}_X(s) - r \;=\; \frac{(s-r)\,(s^3 - 2rs^2 - 2r^2 s + 2r^3)}{3s^3 + 2X}\,.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The quartic factor $\Psi(s) = s^3 - 2rs^2 - 2r^2 s + 2r^3$ satisfies $\Psi(r) = -r^3$. Near $s = r$, this gives a local contraction ratio $\lambda_3 = -r^3/(3r^3+2r^3) = -1/5$, confirming the universal linear convergence rate of Theorem~\ref{thm:derivative}. The negative sign explains the characteristic alternating approach pattern. When Steffensen acceleration is applied (Section~\ref{sec:classical}), this linear rate is converted to quadratic convergence.

\subsection{The Thue--Pandrosion Generator}
When $(A,B) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ and $X \in \mathbb{Z}$, the iteration produces integer sequences:
\[
  A' = A(A^3 + 4XB^3), \qquad B' = B(3A^3 + 2XB^3).
\]
Crucially, the integer residual $M_n = A_n^3 - X B_n^3$ has a \emph{multiplicative} structure:

\begin{theorem}[Pell--Pandrosion Identity, \texttt{PellDiophantine.lean}]\label{thm:pell}
For all $X, A, B \in \mathbb{Z}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pell}
  A_{n+1}^3 - X\, B_{n+1}^3 \;=\; (A_n^3 - X B_n^3) \cdot \bigl(A_n^9 - 14X A_n^6 B_n^3 - 20X^2 A_n^3 B_n^6 + 8X^3 B_n^9\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This is the integer counterpart of the real conservation identity~\eqref{eq:conservation}. It shows that the ``error term'' $A^3 - XB^3$ is \emph{multiplicative}: each iteration multiplies it by a degree-9 polynomial factor. In other words, if $A_0^3 - X B_0^3 = M_0$, then $M_n = M_0 \cdot \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \Phi_k$, where each $\Phi_k$ is an explicit polynomial in the current state.

The Thue--Pandrosion progression is then defined by the numerator-denominator evolution:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thue}
  \boxed{(A')^3 - X(B')^3 \;=\; (A^3 - XB^3) \cdot (A^9 - 14\,A^6 X B^3 - 20\,A^3 X^2 B^6 + 8X^3 B^9).}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Diophantine theory]
This identity shows that the Pandrosion map preserves the cubic Thue form $A^3 - XB^3$ multiplicatively: the new error is always an exact algebraic multiple of the old one. This creates an \emph{explicit} generator of integer solutions to equations of the form $A^3 - XB^3 = M\cdot\Phi$. While the structure is reminiscent of classical Thue--Pell theory, our result is an algebraic identity about one specific recurrence, not a general decidability result for Diophantine equations.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Coprimality Isolation Identities}
The following identities show how the Pandrosion iteration constrains prime factor propagation through explicit algebraic relations.

\begin{theorem}[Deep 33: Coprimality Isolation Identities]

\begin{align}
  B \cdot A' - A \cdot B' &= 2AB(XB^3 - A^3), \label{eq:abc1}\\
  2A \cdot B' - B \cdot A' &= 5A^4 B, \label{eq:abc2}\\
  3B \cdot A' - A \cdot B' &= 10XAB^4. \label{eq:abc3}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Prime Isolation Bound]
If a prime $p$ divides both $A'$ and $B'$, then by \eqref{eq:abc2} and \eqref{eq:abc3}, $p$ must simultaneously divide $5A^4B$ and $10XAB^4$. Without assuming strict coprimality of $A$ and $B$, this implies $p \mid 5A \lor p \mid 10XB \lor p \mid \gcd(A,B)$, limiting the propagation of new common prime factors at each discrete step of the integer sequences.
\end{corollary}

\section{Lipschitz-Type Cross-State Factorisation}
\label{sec:lipschitz}
%===========================================================

\subsection{Algebraic Stability Under Perturbation}
A natural question for any iteration is: how does the output change when the input is perturbed? If $A$ and $B$ are two initial states, we can study the ``cross-difference'' $U(A)V(B) - U(B)V(A)$ to understand how the iteration separates or contracts nearby trajectories.

\begin{theorem}[Deep 32: Cross-State Factorisation]
Over any commutative ring:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lipschitz}
  \boxed{U(A) \cdot V(B) - U(B) \cdot V(A) \;=\; (A - B) \cdot \Phi(A, B, X),}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi(A,B,X) = 3A^3B^3 + 2X(A^3 + A^2B + AB^2 + B^3) - 12XAB(A+B) + 8X^2$.
\end{theorem}

This identity shows that the cross-difference is always divisible by $(A-B)$, with the quotient being an explicit polynomial. When specialised to $\mathbb{R}$ (with $V$ non-vanishing), it implies that the rational map $s \mapsto U(s)/V(s)$ is locally Lipschitz bounded by a polynomial of the state parameters, ensuring analytic smoothness.

\section{Effective Rational Contraction}
\label{sec:irrationality}
%===========================================================

Deep~28 gives an explicit contraction factor for the Pandrosion iteration applied to rational approximants, demonstrating how the error scales predictably:

\begin{theorem}[Deep 28: Effective Irrationality Proximity]
For $(A,B) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ with the Pandrosion iteration step:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:irrationality}
  \left(\frac{A'}{B'}\right)^{\!3} - X \;=\; \left[\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{\!3} - X\right] \cdot \frac{(A/B)^9 - 14(A/B)^6 X - \cdots + 8X^3}{\bigl(3(A/B)^3 + 2X\bigr)^3}\,.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This provides an \emph{explicit} contraction factor for each iteration step within the Pandrosion sequence. Note that this is an identity about the behaviour of one specific recurrence, not a general effective irrationality measure for arbitrary Diophantine approximations. The effectivity here is limited to approximations generated by the Pandrosion map itself.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%===========================================================

We have introduced the Pandrosion iteration, an original rational map for computing cube roots, and established a systematic corpus of exact algebraic identities governing its behaviour across multiple algebraic structures. The key contribution is methodological: by formalising every identity in Lean~4, we provide a fully machine-verified foundation with zero unproven assertions.

\subsection*{What has been proven}
The identities themselves are exact and unconditional: residual factorisation over $\mathbb{R}$ (Deep~25), multiplicative Thue progression over $\mathbb{Z}$ (Deep~26), matrilinear error factorisation over rings (Deep~24), effective contraction for rational approximants (Deep~28), fixed-point characterisation on $\mathbb{C}$ (Deep~29), commutativity preservation for matrices (Deep~30), Hermitian preservation (Deep~31), Lipschitz-type cross-state factorisation (Deep~32), and coprimality isolation identities (Deep~33). Each of these is a \emph{certified algebraic fact}.

\subsection*{What has not been proven}
We do \emph{not} claim to have resolved any generalized theorems to which these identities thematically relate. The gap between an algebraic identity about a specific iteration and a resolution of highly generalized classification problems is immense. Our identities provide interesting structural constraints on the Pandrosion map alone.

\subsection*{The value of the work}
The genuine contribution lies in: (1)~a rational cube-root iteration, algebraically distinct from Newton, Halley, and the entire Chebyshev--Halley family, with a formally proved universal convergence rate of exactly $1/5$; (2)~a rich algebraic structure that propagates cleanly across real, integer, complex, ring, and matrix domains; (3)~the numerical observation that the complex-plane basins appear free of fractal boundaries, combined with the formal geometric proof that the real Multi-Start algorithm generates strictly connected Vorono\"i basins; and (4)~a methodology of formal verification (377~theorems, zero \texttt{sorry}) applied to iterative numerical analysis.

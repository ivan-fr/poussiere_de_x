\begin{abstract}
We present a Lean~4 / Mathlib v4.7.0 development of a multi-start
variant of the Pandrosion-Steffensen iteration for solving
$z^p = x$ with $x \in \mathbb{C}^\ast$ and $p \geq 1$. The scheme
combines the rational iteration $h_{p,x}(z) = 1 - (x-1)/(x\,S_p(z))$
(where $S_p(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{p-1} z^k$), the Aitken-Steffensen $\Delta^2$
accelerator $\sigma = \mathrm{Aitken}(h)$, and a perturbative
seed $\mathrm{seed}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(z) = \alpha + \varepsilon(z-\alpha)$
whose definition depends on the user-chosen cyclotomic target $\alpha$.

The paper reports three kinds of content, explicitly separated:
\emph{(a)~results that are new in this formalization}, concerning the
universal convergence of the multi-start scheme with an explicit
closed-form iteration count, the Borel-measurability and a.e.\ continuity
of the resulting solution map, and a set of elementary algebraic identities
for the finite series
$\zeta_P(s) := \sum_{k=1}^p P^{\prime}(\alpha_k)^{-s}$;
\emph{(b)~Lean specializations of classical theorems (Banach 1922,
Aitken 1926, Schwarz 1890, Kronecker 1857, Aberth--Ehrlich 1967/1973)
to the Pandrosion setting — these are not new mathematics, only new
formalizations. Three further modules
(\texttt{PandrosionCardano}, \texttt{PandrosionVieta},
\texttt{PandrosionGalois}) record definitional-level identities for the
cyclotomic anchors of $z^p = x$ and are not counted as specializations
of the classical theorems};
\emph{(c)~two short structural observations: a compliance interface
for the Kung--Traub upper bound for $c \geq 3$, and a statement-level
recording of McMullen 1987. Two other classical themes (Lehmer's
Mahler-measure conjecture, Smale's~17th problem) that earlier drafts
discussed have been removed from scope, because the Pandrosion setting
contributes nothing to either}.

The Lean development comprises 121 modules with zero \texttt{sorry} and
an audited dependency closure reducing to
$\{\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}\}$,
verifiable per-theorem via \texttt{\#print axioms}
(see Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}).
The main contribution of the corpus is the formalization itself and the
multi-start convergence analysis of~(a), not any of the engagements
in~(c); readers primarily interested in the mathematics should focus on
Sections~\ref{sec:real-line}--\ref{sec:zeta}.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Historical starting point: Pandrosion's two sequences for $\sqrt[3]{2}$}
\label{sec:historical}

Before stating the general iterator, we recall the two sequences that
Pappus (\textit{Collectio}, Book III) attributes to Pandrosion of
Alexandria for approximating~$\sqrt[3]{2}$. Given an initial
parameter~$u_0 \in (0, 4)$, the construction produces the pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pandrosion-two-sequences}
  u_{n+1} \;=\; \frac{u_n}{\displaystyle 2 \;-\; 2\!\left(\frac{4 - u_n}{4}\right)^{\!3}},
  \qquad
  v_n \;=\; 2 \left(\frac{4 - u_n}{4}\right)^{\!2},
  \qquad
  u_0 \;=\; 0.5.
\end{equation}
The sequence $(u_n)$ is the internal parameter (a length in the Thales
diagram); the sequence $(v_n)$ is the \emph{readout}, converging to
$\sqrt[3]{2}$.

\paragraph{Normalization.} The substitution
$s_n := (4 - u_n)/4 \in (0,1)$ eliminates all constants:
\[
  u_n \;=\; 4(1 - s_n),
  \qquad
  v_n \;=\; 2 s_n^{2},
  \qquad
  u_{n+1} \;=\; \frac{4(1 - s_n)}{2(1 - s_n^{3})} \;=\; \frac{2}{1 + s_n + s_n^{2}},
\]
which turns~\eqref{eq:pandrosion-two-sequences} into the single rational
iteration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pandrosion-s-iter}
  s_{n+1} \;=\; 1 - \frac{u_{n+1}}{4}
          \;=\; \frac{2 s_n^{2} + 2 s_n + 1}{2 s_n^{2} + 2 s_n + 2}.
\end{equation}
The fixed point of~\eqref{eq:pandrosion-s-iter} is $s^\ast = 2^{-1/3}$,
giving $v^\ast = 2 \cdot 2^{-2/3} = 2^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{2}$, and the local
convergence is \emph{linear} with ratio
\[
  \lambda_{3,2}
  \;=\; \frac{(\sqrt[3]{2} - 1)(\sqrt[3]{2} + 2)}{\sqrt[3]{4} + \sqrt[3]{2} + 1}
  \;\approx\; 0.2202.
\]

\paragraph{Recognition of the general form.} Writing
$x = 2$, $p = 3$, and $S_3(s) = 1 + s + s^{2}$, the
iteration~\eqref{eq:pandrosion-s-iter} rearranges to
\[
  s_{n+1} \;=\; 1 - \frac{1}{2(1 + s_n + s_n^{2})}
           \;=\; 1 - \frac{x - 1}{x \cdot S_p(s_n)}\bigg|_{p = 3,\, x = 2}.
\]
This is exactly the iterator $h_{p,x}$ introduced below, at
$(p, x) = (3, 2)$. The fixed-point equation $s^\ast = 1 - (x-1)/(x\,S_p(s^\ast))$
rearranges to $x \cdot (s^\ast)^p = 1$, i.e.\ $s^\ast = x^{-1/p}$, and
the readout $v^\ast = x \cdot (s^\ast)^{p-1} = x^{1/p}$ recovers the
target $p$-th root. The historical
construction~\eqref{eq:pandrosion-two-sequences} is therefore the
$(p, x) = (3, 2)$ instance of a one-parameter family defined in~\eqref{eq:pandrosion-h}.

\subsection{The general iterator $h_{p,x}$}
\label{sec:general-iterator}

On $\mathbb{C}^\ast$ with $p \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}$, the Pandrosion
iterator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pandrosion-h}
  h_{p,x}(z) \;:=\; 1 - \frac{x-1}{x \cdot S_p(z)},
  \qquad
  S_p(z) \;:=\; \sum_{k=0}^{p-1} z^k,
\end{equation}
whose fixed points are exactly the $p$-th roots of $1/x$:
$h_{p,x}(s) = s \iff s^p = 1/x$. The historical
case~\eqref{eq:pandrosion-two-sequences} is the instance
$(p, x) = (3, 2)$. Two constructions operate on top of~$h_{p,x}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item an \emph{algebraic acceleration}, the Aitken $\Delta^2$ transform
  $\sigma_{p,x} := \mathrm{Aitken}(h_{p,x})$, which upgrades linear to
  quadratic convergence without computing derivatives
  (Section~\ref{sec:real-line});
\item a \emph{seeded multi-start} in~$\mathbb{C}$, placing $z_0$ inside
  a controlled disk around a user-chosen cyclotomic anchor before
  iteration begins (Section~\ref{sec:multistart}).
\end{itemize}
Both are used below. We first analyze $h_{p,x}$ on the real line
(Section~\ref{sec:real-line}), where the closed-form contraction ratio
and preconditionings are cleanest, then extend to the complex case
via multi-start.

\subsection{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Rational iterations for $p$-th roots are classical. Newton's method applied
to $f(u) = u^p - x$, Halley's method, and the Schröder (1870) and
König (1884) families supply iterations of arbitrary order with explicit
asymptotic constants; Traub's \emph{Iterative Methods for the Solution
of Equations} (1964) is the canonical systematic treatment of fixed-point
iterations and their acceleration, and Ortega--Rheinboldt (1970) treats
the multidimensional analogue. Aitken--Steffensen $\Delta^2$ acceleration
is standard textbook material~\cite{numrecipes}. The cyclotomic identities
in Section~\ref{sec:zeta} are elementary consequences of the geometric-sum
vanishing $\sum_k \omega^{ks} = 0$ on roots of unity, which is used in
every undergraduate treatment of cyclotomic polynomials.

What is specific to this paper is: (i) the rational iteration~$h_{p,x}$
arising from the Pandrosion construction, which is \emph{not} the Newton
iteration of any polynomial and has order one rather than two; (ii) the
closed-form ratio $\lambda_{p,x}$ and the preconditioning built on its
monotonicity; (iii) the multi-start seed of~\S\ref{sec:seed} together
with the measurability/a.e.-continuity analysis of the induced solution
map; (iv) the Lean~4 formalization itself, which is the main deliverable.
We do not claim the iteration itself, or the ancillary identities, are
novel mathematics; the contribution is to assemble them in a verified
formal framework and to record the specific Pandrosion parameters.

\subsection{On naming and scope}

A remark on terminology. We define
$\zeta_P(s) := \sum_{k=1}^p P^{\prime}(\alpha_k)^{-s}$ as a finite series
of $p$ complex terms. All the identities we prove for it
(including $\zeta_P(1) = 0$ and a closed-form for
$\prod_k P^{\prime}(\alpha_k)$) reduce to the geometric-sum identity on
roots of unity and are elementary. We occasionally use a vocabulary
reminiscent of the Riemann zeta function (``integer zeros of $\zeta_P$'')
because the shape of the identities mirrors it, but we do \emph{not}
claim any number-theoretic content and the analogy should be read as
typographic, not mathematical. In particular, nothing in this paper
bears on the Riemann Hypothesis or on any transcendental question.

(The label ``Niveau~5'' used in Theorem~\ref{thm:niveau5} and in the
Lean module naming is internal bookkeeping for the corpus and has no
external attestation; see the footnote in~\S\ref{sec:multistart}.)

\subsection{Reading order}

Sections~\ref{sec:multistart}--\ref{sec:analysis} contain the multi-start

\section{The Pandrosion iteration on the real line}
\label{sec:real-line}
%======================================================================

This section studies $h_{p,x}$ on $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, where every
quantity admits a closed form. The results of independent interest are
three: a rational expression for the linear contraction ratio
$\lambda_{p,x}$ depending only on the target (\S\ref{sec:contraction-ratio}),
an optimal derivative-free starting point
(\S\ref{sec:optimal-start}), and a scaling preconditioning that
collapses the iteration count for large~$x$ (\S\ref{sec:scaling}).
Sections~\ref{sec:aitken-delta2} and~\ref{sec:quadratic-local} then
show that Aitken's $\Delta^2$ turns $h_{p,x}$ into the quadratically
convergent $\sigma_{p,x}$ with a fully explicit local bound; this is
the tool that drives the complex analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:multistart}.

Throughout, $x > 1$ and $p \geq 2$; $\alpha := x^{1/p} > 1$ is the
target $p$-th root and $s^\ast := 1/\alpha = x^{-1/p}$ is the unique
fixed point of $h_{p,x}$ in $(0, 1)$. The readout is
$v_n := x \cdot s_n^{p-1}$, converging to $v^\ast = \alpha$.

\subsection{Closed-form contraction ratio}
\label{sec:contraction-ratio}

\begin{theorem}[Closed-form contraction ratio]\label{thm:lambda-closed}
For $x > 1$, $p \geq 2$, the iteration $s_{n+1} = h_{p,x}(s_n)$ is
linearly convergent at $s^\ast$ with ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda-closed-form}
  \lambda_{p,x}
  \;=\; h_{p,x}^{\prime}(s^\ast)
  \;=\; \frac{(\alpha - 1)\,\sum_{k=1}^{p-1} k\,\alpha^{p-1-k}}
             {\sum_{k=0}^{p-1} \alpha^k},
  \qquad
  \alpha = x^{1/p}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\lambda_{p,x} \in (0, 1)$ is a rational function of the
target~$\alpha$ alone.
\end{theorem}

Direct computation: $h_{p,x}^\prime(s) = (x-1) S_p^\prime(s) / (x S_p(s)^2)$,
evaluated at $s^\ast = 1/\alpha$ and simplified using $x = \alpha^p$ and
$x S_p(s^\ast) = \alpha/(\alpha-1)$.

\paragraph{Why this matters.} The number of iterations to reach
precision $\delta$ is governed by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iter-count}
  n_\delta \;\approx\;
  \left\lceil \frac{\log(\varepsilon_0/\delta)}{\log(1/\lambda_{p,x})} \right\rceil,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_0 := |v_0 - \alpha|.
\end{equation}
Two levers for reducing $n_\delta$ are therefore available: reducing
$\varepsilon_0$ (one-step preconditioning of the argument, \S\ref{sec:optimal-start})
and reducing $\lambda_{p,x}$ (scaling of the target, \S\ref{sec:scaling}).
Thus $\lambda_{p,x}$ encodes the method-target interaction independently
of the starting point.

\paragraph{Monotonicity and limits.} A further computation (carried out in
Lean in the corpus) shows that $\lambda_{p,x}$ is strictly increasing in both
$p$ and~$x$ on $[1, \infty)$. The limits are
\[
  \lim_{x \to \infty} \lambda_{p,x} \;=\; 1^-
  \quad \text{(for fixed $p$)},
  \qquad
  \lim_{p \to \infty} \lambda_{p,x}
   \;=\; 1 - \frac{\ln x}{x - 1}
   \;\in\; (0, 1)
  \quad \text{(for fixed $x > 1$)}.
\]
In particular, for fixed $x$ the ratio does \emph{not} tend to~$1$ as
$p$ grows — it saturates at $1 - \ln(x)/(x-1)$. The scaling
preconditioning of~\S\ref{sec:scaling} is built directly on the
monotonicity in~$x$.

\subsection{Optimal starting point: one-step preconditioning}
\label{sec:optimal-start}

\begin{proposition}[Optimal $\alpha$-free starting point]\label{prop:optimal-start}
Among starting points $s_0 \in (0, 1)$ computable without knowledge
of~$\alpha$, the choice
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal-start}
  s_0^{\mathrm{opt}} \;:=\; h_{p,x}(1) \;=\; 1 - \frac{x - 1}{x\,p}
\end{equation}
minimises the initial residual $|s_0 - s^\ast|$ to leading order.
\end{proposition}

Since $h_{p,x}$ is the method's own one-step update and~$s^\ast$ is
its fixed point, applying $h_{p,x}$ to the naive boundary value
$s = 1$ gives the best single-step estimate of~$s^\ast$ that does not
require $\alpha$. Taylor expansion at $s = 1$ gives
$s^\ast = 1 - (\ln x)/p + O(1/p^2)$, so
$|s_0^{\mathrm{opt}} - s^\ast| = |(x-1)/(xp) - (\ln x)/p| + O(1/p^2)$,
substantially smaller than $|1 - s^\ast|$ or $|1/2 - s^\ast|$ in the
relevant regime.

\subsection{Scaling preconditioning: exploiting monotonicity}
\label{sec:scaling}

For large~$x$, the contraction ratio $\lambda_{p,x}$ is close to~$1$
and~\eqref{eq:iter-count} explodes. The monotonicity
$x \mapsto \lambda_{p,x}$ is however the basis of a simple remedy.

\begin{proposition}[Scaling reduction]\label{prop:scaling}
Let $A \in \mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}$ satisfy $A^{1/p} \in \mathbb{N}$ and
$x^\prime := x/A \in [1, (1 + 1/A^{1/p})^p)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling-factorization}
  x^{1/p} \;=\; A^{1/p} \cdot (x^\prime)^{1/p},
\end{equation}
so that computing $x^{1/p}$ reduces to computing $(x^\prime)^{1/p}$ and
multiplying by the integer $A^{1/p}$. Taking $A = \lfloor x^{1/p} \rfloor^p$
makes $x^\prime$ close to~$1$ and the ratio $\lambda_{p,x^\prime}$ close
to~$0$.
\end{proposition}

The saving compounds over every iteration: with
$\varepsilon_0^\prime \ll \varepsilon_0$ and
$\log(1/\lambda_{p,x^\prime}) \gg \log(1/\lambda_{p,x})$,
\eqref{eq:iter-count} drops by a factor of several tens to several
hundred (Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). This is the real-variable analogue
of the complex multi-start seed of \S\ref{sec:multistart}, which
places $z_0$ close to an anchor by contracting towards it rather than
by rescaling the argument.

\begin{remark}[Double preconditioning]
The two accelerations combine multiplicatively:
\eqref{eq:optimal-start} reduces~$\varepsilon_0$ by a fixed factor,
\eqref{eq:scaling-factorization} reduces $1/\log(1/\lambda_{p,x})$ by
an $x$-dependent factor, and~\eqref{eq:iter-count} benefits from both
simultaneously.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Aitken $\Delta^2$: from linear to quadratic convergence}
\label{sec:aitken-delta2}

The linear convergence of $h_{p,x}$ with explicit ratio $\lambda_{p,x}$
is exactly the input required by the Aitken $\Delta^2$ transform.

\begin{proposition}[Aitken $\Delta^2$ on geometric sequences]
\label{prop:aitken}
For any geometric sequence $s_n = s^\ast + C \cdot r^n$ with $r \neq 0, 1$
and $C \neq 0$,
\[
  s_n - \frac{(s_{n+1} - s_n)^2}{s_{n+2} - 2 s_{n+1} + s_n}
  \;=\; s^\ast \qquad \text{for every } n.
\]
\end{proposition}

Applied at each step to the Pandrosion iterates, this yields the
derivative-free accelerator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:steffensen-sigma}
  \sigma_{p,x}(z) \;:=\; z - \frac{(h_{p,x}(z) - z)^2}
                               {h_{p,x}(h_{p,x}(z)) - 2 h_{p,x}(z) + z}
\end{equation}
(one $\Delta^2$ step per iterate; compare \cite{aitken, steffensen}).

\end{figure}

\subsection{A quadratic local bound at the super-attractive fixed point}
\label{sec:quadratic-local}

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic local bound]\label{prop:quadratic-local}
The map $\sigma_{p,x}$ has a super-attractive fixed point at every
$p$-th root of $1/x$: $\sigma_{p,x}(s^\ast) = s^\ast$ and
$\sigma_{p,x}^{\prime}(s^\ast) = 0$. On a neighbourhood
$B(s^\ast, R_\sigma)$,
\[
  |\sigma_{p,x}(s) - s^\ast|
  \;\leq\; K_\sigma \cdot |s - s^\ast|^2,
\]
where the constants admit the explicit forms
\[
  K_\sigma \;=\; \left|\frac{h''_{p,x}(s^\ast)}{2\,(1 - \lambda_{p,x})}\right|,
  \qquad
  R_\sigma \;=\; \min\!\bigl(1/(2K_\sigma),\; \mathrm{dist}(s^\ast, \mathrm{sing}(\sigma_{p,x}))\bigr),
\]
so that $K_\sigma \cdot R_\sigma \leq 1/2$. The same bound holds
in~$\mathbb{C}$ on $B(\gamma_k, R_\sigma)$ for every cyclotomic anchor
$\gamma_k$. The explicit formulas are formalized in
\texttt{Pandrosion/Core/QuadraticBound.lean} and
\texttt{SteffensenQuadraticBound.lean}.
\end{proposition}

This is the Schwarz-Pick-type estimate that the multi-start scheme in
$\mathbb{C}$ exploits: once $z$ is inside $B(\gamma_k, R/2)$, successive
iterates contract quadratically and the $\log\log$ iteration count
of~\eqref{thm:eff-kt} follows.

\label{sec:cyclotomic}

\subsection{Cyclotomic anchor family}

\begin{definition}[Cyclotomic anchors]\label{def:cyc-anchor}
For $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}^\ast$ and $p \geq 1$, let
\[
  \gamma_k := \alpha \cdot e^{2\pi i k / p}, \qquad k = 0, 1, \dots, p-1.
\]
\end{definition}

When $\alpha^p = 1/x$, the $\gamma_k$ are the $p$ distinct solutions of
$z^p = 1/x$.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig02_cyclotomic_anchors.png}
\caption{Cyclotomic anchor families for $p \in \{3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13\}$ at
$x = 2$. The anchors lie on the circle of radius $|\alpha| = 2^{-1/p}$,
and the cyclic action $\omega \cdot \gamma_k = \gamma_{k+1 \bmod p}$
generates the splitting field.}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Cyclic permutation of the anchors}

By direct expansion of Definition~\ref{def:cyc-anchor}, $\omega\cdot\gamma_k
= \gamma_{(k+1)\bmod p}$ for every $k$ (with $\omega^p = 1$). Likewise
$\sum_k \gamma_k = 0$ and $\prod_k \gamma_k = (-1)^{p-1}\cdot x$. These
are definitional consequences for $z^p = x$, not specializations of the
Galois or Vieta theorems; we use them freely in the sequel.

\section{Universal multi-start convergence}
\label{sec:multistart}
%======================================================================

We now extend the iteration to~$\mathbb{C}$. The linear-to-quadratic
acceleration of Section~\ref{sec:aitken-delta2} applies verbatim to the
complex iterator~\eqref{eq:pandrosion-h}, giving the Steffensen
map~$\sigma_{p,x}$ of~\eqref{eq:steffensen-sigma} on all of~$\mathbb{C}$.
What is genuinely new over the real-line case is the need to control
\emph{which} cyclotomic anchor the iteration converges to: in~$\mathbb{C}$,
$\sigma_{p,x}$ has $p$ attractive fixed points, the Julia set
separating their basins is generically non-trivial, and a classical
impossibility result of McMullen~\cite{mcmullen} rules out purely
iterative universal root finders for $p \geq 3$ in a strong
sense. The seed of \S\ref{sec:seed} is designed precisely to exit the
hypotheses of~\cite{mcmullen}.

\subsection{The multi-start seed}
\label{sec:seed}

\begin{definition}[Multi-start seed]\label{def:seed}
For a cyclotomic anchor $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}^\ast$ with $\alpha^p = 1/x$
and $z \in \mathbb{C}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multistart-seed}
  \mathrm{seed}_{\alpha, \varepsilon}(z)
  \;:=\; \alpha + \varepsilon \cdot (z - \alpha),
  \qquad
  \varepsilon
  \;:=\; \frac{R_\sigma(x, p, \alpha)}{2\,\|z - \alpha\|}
  \quad (\varepsilon := 1 \text{ if } z = \alpha),
\end{equation}
where $R_\sigma(x, p, \alpha)$ is the explicit quadratic-contraction
radius from Proposition~\ref{prop:quadratic-local}. The seed always
lies in $\overline{B}(\alpha, R_\sigma/2)$.
\end{definition}

The seed plays the role in~$\mathbb{C}$ that the preconditionings
of~\S\S\ref{sec:optimal-start}--\ref{sec:scaling} play on $\mathbb{R}$:
it places the argument inside the quadratic-contraction disk of the
target \emph{before} the iteration begins. It is $\alpha$-dependent by
design, and this dependency is what takes the scheme out of the
hypotheses of~\cite{mcmullen}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig01_multistart_basins.png}
\caption{Structure of the multi-start seed. Each point $z \in \mathbb{C}$
is mapped to $\mathrm{seed}_{\alpha,\varepsilon_\mathrm{seed}}(z)$,
which lies in $\overline{B}(\alpha, R_\sigma/2)$ regardless of $z$;
the iteration is then run from the seed. Colour indicates the number
of iterations needed to reach precision $10^{-6}$ after the seed is
applied. The figure illustrates how
seed~\eqref{eq:multistart-seed} places the scheme outside the
hypotheses of~\cite{mcmullen} by making the iterate entry point
$\alpha$-dependent; we explicitly do \emph{not} compare with Newton's
basin structure here, since the two schemes do not solve the same
problem (Newton is parameter-free, multi-start is $\alpha$-targeted).}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Universal multi-start theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Universal multi-start convergence]\label{thm:multistart}
Let $x \in \mathbb{C}^\ast$, $p \geq 1$, $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}^\ast$ with
$\alpha^p = 1/x$ and $S_p(\alpha) \neq 0$. Then for every $z \in \mathbb{C}$
and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exist $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seed}} > 0$
and $N \in \mathbb{N}$ such that
\[
  \forall n \geq N: \quad
  \big\|\sigma_{p,x}^n(\mathrm{seed}_{\alpha,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seed}}}(z))
        - \alpha\big\| \leq \varepsilon.
\]
The construction is explicit: $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seed}}
= R_\sigma(x,p,\alpha) / (2\,\|z - \alpha\|)$ when $z \neq \alpha$, and
$N$ is given by Theorem~\ref{thm:eff-kt} below.
\end{theorem}

The content is: the seed is in $\overline{B}(\alpha, R_\sigma/2)$, the
iteration stays in $B(\alpha, R_\sigma)$, and a quadratic-contraction
argument gives the iteration count.

\begin{remark}[The hypothesis $S_p(\alpha) \neq 0$]
The non-vanishing condition $S_p(\alpha) \neq 0$ rules out the degenerate
case where the denominator of $h_{p,x}$ vanishes at the anchor.
Since $S_p(\alpha) = (\alpha^p - 1)/(\alpha - 1) = (1/x - 1)/(\alpha - 1)$ for
$\alpha^p = 1/x$ and $\alpha \neq 1$, this is an event of codimension at
least one in the $(x, p)$-parameter space and in particular holds
generically; it fails only for specific arithmetic combinations (for
instance when $\alpha$ is a non-trivial $p$-th root of unity, which
requires $x = 1$). The Lean corpus carries this hypothesis explicitly in
the statement.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Explicit iteration count}

\begin{definition}[Effective iteration count]
For $K, R, \varepsilon > 0$ with $K R < 1$ and $K \varepsilon < 1$,
\[
  N_{\mathrm{eff}}(K, R, \varepsilon) :=
  \log_2 \big\lceil \log(K \varepsilon) / \log(K R) \big\rceil_+ + 1,
\]
with $\lceil \cdot \rceil_+$ the natural-number ceiling clamped at $0$.
The hypotheses $K R < 1$ and $K \varepsilon < 1$ ensure both logarithms
are negative and their ratio is well-defined and non-negative.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit iteration count]\label{thm:eff-kt}
For any quadratically contracting sequence $e_{n+1} \leq K \cdot e_n^2$
with $e_0 \leq R$, $K \cdot R < 1$, and $\varepsilon < R$, every iterate
$k \geq N_{\mathrm{eff}}(K, R, \varepsilon)$ satisfies $e_k \leq \varepsilon$.
Asymptotically $N_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim \log_2 \log(1/\varepsilon)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig03_loglog_convergence.png}
\caption{Left: error $\|\sigma^n(\mathrm{seed}) - \alpha\|$ vs iteration
for $p \in \{3, 4, 5, 7\}$, $x = 2$, $z_0 = 1 + 0.5i$. Right: iteration
count $N(\varepsilon)$ vs target precision for the same setup. The
dashed line is $\log(1/\varepsilon)$; it is shown only for visual

\end{figure}

\subsection{An a.e.-convergence statement for the plain iteration}

It is natural to ask whether the plain Steffensen iteration
$\sigma_{p,x}$ converges for almost every starting point to the
principal anchor~$\alpha_0$:
\[
  \forall^{\mathrm{a.e.}}\,z \in \mathbb{C}: \quad
  \sigma_{p,x}^n(z) \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} \alpha_0.
\]
Numerical experiments at $(x, p) = (2, 3)$ (Python sweep over
$[-3, 3]^2$ at $200 \times 200$ resolution) show that roughly
$0.015\%$ of initial points converge instead to a non-principal
anchor, so the plain-iteration version of the statement is
\emph{false}.\footnote{In the Lean corpus this a.e.-statement is
tracked internally under the label ``Niveau~5'' (modules
\texttt{Niveau5Master}, \texttt{Niveau5StrictMultiStart}, etc.). The
label is ours; it does not correspond to any externally attested
conjecture, and there is no ``Niveau~4'' hidden elsewhere — it is
simply internal bookkeeping.}

\begin{theorem}[Multi-start version]\label{thm:niveau5}
Under the standard hypotheses on $(x, p, \alpha)$, the multi-start
reformulation
\[
  \forall\,z \in \mathbb{C}, \exists\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{seed}} > 0:\quad
  \sigma_{p,x}^n(\alpha + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{seed}} \cdot (z - \alpha))
  \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} \alpha
\]
holds unconditionally.
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:multistart} restated. The content is not that
the reformulation is ``solved'': it is that by conditioning on the
choice of target anchor $\alpha$ and adjusting the seed accordingly,
the convergence problem decouples from the dynamics of $\sigma_{p,x}$
on all of $\mathbb{C}$. What the reformulation gives up, compared to
the plain-iteration statement, is universality: the seed depends on
$\alpha$ and can therefore not be interpreted as a ``method that
converges regardless of target''.

\subsection{Arbitrary target selector}

\begin{theorem}[Target selection]\label{thm:galois-equiv}
For every function $S : \mathbb{C} \to \mathrm{Fin}\,p$, every
$z \in \mathbb{C}$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, the multi-start scheme
with target $\gamma_{S(z)}$ converges to $\gamma_{S(z)}$ with an
iteration count of the form of Theorem~\ref{thm:eff-kt}.
\end{theorem}

Three selectors are implemented: constant (\texttt{principal\_selector}),
Voronoï-nearest (\texttt{nearest\_anchor\_selector}), angular sector.

\section{Measurability and a.e.\ continuity of the solution map}
\label{sec:analysis}
%======================================================================

The \texttt{nearest\_anchor\_selector} of Section~\ref{sec:multistart}
uses \texttt{Classical.choose} on \texttt{Finset.exists\_min\_image};
this is not a priori measurable.

\begin{definition}[Borel selector]
\[
  S_{\mathrm{Borel}}(\alpha, p, z) := \min{}'
  \big(\,\mathrm{argmin}_{k \in \mathrm{Fin}\,p}\,\|\gamma_k - z\|\,\big).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Borel measurability]\label{thm:borel}
The map $S_{\mathrm{Borel}}$ is Borel-measurable. Consequently the
solution map $z \mapsto \gamma_{S_{\mathrm{Borel}}(z)}$ is Borel-measurable
$\mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$.
\end{theorem}

The proof shows that each level set is a finite Boolean combination of
closed half-planes $\{z : \|z - \gamma_s\| \leq \|z - \gamma_t\|\}$.

\begin{definition}[Voronoï boundary and its complement]
$\mathrm{VorBdy}(\alpha, p) := \mathrm{VoronoiBoundary}(\gamma_0, \dots, \gamma_{p-1})$,
$\mathrm{VorInt}(\alpha, p) := \mathbb{C} \setminus \mathrm{VorBdy}(\alpha, p)$.
\end{definition}

These are Voronoï-geometric objects, not the classical Fatou--Julia sets
of $\sigma_{p,x}$. Earlier drafts of this corpus used the names
``Pandrosion Julia / Fatou set'' for them; this was a poor choice of
vocabulary, because it suggests a relationship to Fatou--Julia dynamics
of $\sigma_{p,x}$ that we do not establish. The theorems below are
statements about Voronoï geometry of the cyclotomic anchor set, nothing
more.

\begin{theorem}[Voronoï boundary is Lebesgue-null]\label{thm:julia-null}
$\mathrm{vol}\big(\mathrm{VorBdy}(\alpha, p)\big) = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A.e.\ continuity]\label{thm:ae-continuous}
The Borel solution map is continuous at every $z \in \mathrm{VorInt}(\alpha, p)$.
Combined with Theorem~\ref{thm:julia-null}, it is continuous at
Lebesgue-a.e.\ $z$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig04_voronoi_julia_null.png}
\caption{Voronoï decomposition of $\mathbb{C}$ for cyclotomic anchors at
$p = 4$ and $p = 7$, $x = 2$. Each colored region $V_k$ is the basin of
attraction of $\gamma_k$ under the selector. The red frontier is the
Voronoï boundary of the cyclotomic anchor set; it is Lebesgue-null.}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig08_fatou_julia.png}
\caption{Left: Voronoï basins as the structural reference, computed
from the nearest-anchor criterion only (no dynamics). Right: number
of multi-start iterations needed to reach precision $10^{-8}$; it is
bounded and essentially flat over the window, in line with
Theorem~\ref{thm:eff-kt}.}
\end{figure}

Together, Theorems~\ref{thm:multistart}, \ref{thm:borel},
\ref{thm:julia-null} and~\ref{thm:ae-continuous} give the analytic
character of the multi-start solution map in our framework:
measurable everywhere, continuous outside the Voronoï boundary of the
cyclotomic anchor set, with that boundary Lebesgue-null.

\section{Elementary identities on the derivative values at cyclotomic roots}
\label{sec:zeta}
%======================================================================

For $P(z) = z^p - 1/x$ with $\alpha^p = 1/x$ and cyclotomic anchors
$\alpha_k = \gamma_k$ (Definition~\ref{def:cyc-anchor}), one has
$P'(\alpha_k) = p\,\alpha_k^{p-1} = p/(x\,\alpha_k)$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pandrosion-zeta}
  \zeta_P(s) := \sum_{k=1}^p P'(\alpha_k)^{-s}
             = (x/p)^{s}\sum_{k=1}^p \alpha_k^{s}.
\end{equation}
Every identity used elsewhere in the corpus about the values
$P'(\alpha_k)$ reduces to the geometric-sum vanishing
$\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\omega^{ks} = 0$ for $\omega = e^{2\pi i/p}$ and
$p \nmid s$. We record the three forms that Lean modules cite directly.

\begin{proposition}[Three elementary identities]\label{thm:zeta-all}
Let $p \geq 2$ and $x \neq 0$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] For every $s \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $1 \leq s \leq p - 1$,
    \[
      \zeta_P(s) = \sum_{k=1}^p P'(\alpha_k)^{-s} = 0.
    \]
    In particular $\zeta_P(1) = 0$.
  \item[(ii)] For every $m \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $0 \leq m \leq p - 2$,
    $\sum_{k=1}^p \alpha_k^m / P'(\alpha_k) = 0$; for $m = p - 1$,
    $\sum_{k=1}^p \alpha_k^{p-1}/P'(\alpha_k) = 1$.
  \item[(iii)] $\prod_{k=1}^p P'(\alpha_k) = (-1)^{p-1}\,p^p/x^{p-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
Items~(i) and~(ii) are the same statement under $s \leftrightarrow m+1$
via $1/P'(\alpha_k) = x\alpha_k/p$; item~(iii) is the product
counterpart. All three follow from $\sum_k \omega^{ks} = 0$ for
$p \nmid s$.
\end{proposition}

We keep this section deliberately short: the identities are logged
because the Lean modules
\texttt{PandrosionZeta.lean},
\texttt{PandrosionHigherVanishing.lean}, and
\texttt{PandrosionSpectralDeterminant.lean} cite them by name, not
because there is substantial mathematics here. The shape of~(i)--(ii)
is reminiscent of vanishing identities for classical arithmetic zeta
functions, but the analogy is typographic only and no number-theoretic
claim is made.

The corpus contains Lean formalizations of a number of classical
theorems, \emph{specialized} to the Pandrosion setting. The purpose is
to have everything in one formal framework; the mathematical statements
are classical. The following table summarizes what is formalized where.

\S113 & Aitken-Steffensen (1926/1933); Schwarz (1890) & Acceleration identity; quadratic local bound. \\

to make the full Pandrosion framework self-contained in Lean. The
corresponding Lean modules are short specializations, not independent
developments of the classical theorems.

\paragraph{Definitional-level items (not listed above).} Three further
modules, \texttt{PandrosionCardano} (\S121), \texttt{PandrosionVieta}
(\S122), and \texttt{PandrosionGalois} (\S119), record the identities
$\gamma_k = \alpha\omega^k$ for $p = 3$, $e_1 = 0$ and $e_p = (-1)^{p-1}x$,
and $\omega\cdot\gamma_k = \gamma_{(k+1)\bmod p}$ respectively. For the
monomial family $z^p = x$ all three are direct expansions of
Definition~\ref{def:cyc-anchor} and do not rely on the classical Cardano,
Vieta, or Galois theorems as such. We therefore do not count them as
specializations of those theorems; they remain in the Lean corpus as
named lemmas for downstream convenience only.

A representative example.

\begin{theorem}[Multi-start orbit has no non-trivial cycle]
For every $(x, p, \alpha)$ in the hypotheses and every seed
$\mathrm{seed} \in B(\alpha, R_\alpha/2)$, the orbit
$(\sigma_{p,x}^n(\mathrm{seed}))_n$ has no non-trivial cycle: if
$\sigma_{p,x}^n(\mathrm{seed}) = \sigma_{p,x}^m(\mathrm{seed})$ with
$n < m$, then $\sigma_{p,x}^n(\mathrm{seed}) = \alpha$.
\end{theorem}

This is Banach's fixed-point theorem applied to $\sigma_{p,x}$ seen as
a $1/4$-contraction on $B(\alpha, R_\alpha/2)$, itself a consequence
of $K_\alpha \cdot R_\alpha \leq 1/2$.

\section{Short structural observations}
\label{sec:open-problems}
%======================================================================

We record two places (Kung--Traub, McMullen) where the Pandrosion
setting intersects a classical or open problem, and one explicit
out-of-scope note for two further classical themes (Lehmer,
Smale~17). None of the material below constitutes progress on the
underlying problems; each subsection states what the corpus
contributes and, just as importantly, what it does not.

\subsection{Kung--Traub upper bound for $c \geq 3$ (open since 1974)}

The conjecture $q \leq 2^{c-1}$ is proven for $c = 2$. Attainability
$q = 4$ at $c = 3$ is witnessed classically (Jarratt 1969) and in our
formal setting.

\smallskip

\textbf{What the corpus provides.} An interface: every
\texttt{DerivativeFreeMethod} in the corpus carries a
\emph{compliance field} asserting $q \leq 2^{c-1}$ for its own
instance. This is not a proof of the Kung--Traub conjecture, only a
structural assertion that any instance we construct satisfies the
bound. The conjecture itself is recorded as a named \texttt{Prop};
the general case for $c \geq 3$ remains open.

\subsection{McMullen 1987 (recorded but not formalized)}

McMullen's barrier is a classical theorem. A full formal proof in
Lean would require Fatou--Julia and post-critically-finite
complex-dynamic machinery that is not present in Mathlib v4.7.0.

\smallskip

\textbf{What the corpus provides.} The statement is recorded as a
named \texttt{Prop}; it is \emph{not} formally proven.
Independently, Theorem~\ref{thm:multistart} shows that our multi-start
scheme has an $\alpha$-dependent seed and therefore does not fall
under McMullen's hypotheses on purely iterative universal root
finders. This observation places our scheme outside the hypothesis
of~\cite{mcmullen}; it does not reproduce or challenge the theorem.

\subsection{Two classical problems we deliberately do \emph{not} engage with}

We close this section by noting two classical problems that earlier
drafts of the corpus were tempted to discuss: Lehmer's 1933
Mahler-measure conjecture, and Smale's~17th problem on deterministic
polynomial root-finding. In both cases, the only non-vacuous things
the Pandrosion setting offers are a trivial observation (for Lehmer:
that $\beta = x \cdot \alpha$ has Mahler measure $x^{p-1}$, which is
$\geq 2$ for $x \geq 2$, far from the conjecture's extremal regime
near~$1$) and a scope-restricted algorithm (for Smale: the
$\mathcal{O}(\log\log(1/\varepsilon))$ deterministic complexity for
the one-parameter family $z^p = x$, which is not the
coefficient-uniform problem Smale asks about). Since in neither case
is there progress worth reporting, we refrain from a dedicated
subsection and simply record that these two problems lie outside the
scope of the corpus.

The Lean module
\texttt{Pandrosion/Core/PandrosionOpenProblems.lean}
nevertheless carries statement-level \texttt{Prop}s
(\texttt{lehmer\_conjecture\_statement},
\texttt{smale17\_deterministic\_statement},
\texttt{pandrosion\_mahler\_observation}) for historical reasons. These
are not referenced anywhere else in the corpus, produce no theorems,
and should be read as frozen records of the discussion above rather
than as active content. A future cleanup pass will either delete them
or move them into a separate \texttt{archive} namespace.

\subsection{Summary}

No claim is made that any classical problem is solved, or even
substantively advanced. The material in this section consists of a
compliance interface (Kung--Traub) and a recording of a classical
statement (McMullen). Readers primarily interested in the mathematics
should focus on Sections~\ref{sec:real-line}--\ref{sec:zeta}.

%======================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%======================================================================

What this paper reports, concretely, is a Lean 4 development built
around the Pandrosion iterator~$h_{p,x}$ and its multi-start Steffensen
extension for $z^p = x$, together with the following ingredients:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item real-line analysis of~$h_{p,x}$: closed-form contraction ratio
        $\lambda_{p,x} = h'_{p,x}(s^\ast)$
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:lambda-closed}), optimal derivative-free
        starting point $s_0^{\mathrm{opt}} = h_{p,x}(1)$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal-start}), scaling
        preconditioning exploiting monotonicity of~$\lambda$
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:scaling}), and Aitken $\Delta^2$
        upgrade to quadratic convergence with explicit local bound
        (Propositions~\ref{prop:aitken}--\ref{prop:quadratic-local});
  \item a universal convergence theorem for the complex multi-start
        scheme with an explicit closed-form iteration count
        (Theorems~\ref{thm:multistart}, \ref{thm:eff-kt});
  \item a Borel-measurability and a.e.-continuity analysis of the
        induced solution map (Theorems~\ref{thm:borel}--\ref{thm:ae-continuous});
  \item a set of elementary finite-sum identities for the series
        $\zeta_P(s) := \sum_k P^{\prime}(\alpha_k)^{-s}$
        (Section~\ref{sec:zeta}), all reducing to roots-of-unity arithmetic;
  \item Lean specializations of classical theorems to the Pandrosion
        setting (Section~\ref{sec:classical});
  \item two short structural observations (Kung--Traub compliance
        interface, McMullen 1987 statement-level recording) in
        Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}; two further classical themes
        that earlier drafts discussed (Lehmer, Smale 17) have been
        removed as being outside scope.
\end{enumerate}

The paper does not claim progress on any classical open problem. The
main value of the corpus is as a self-contained Lean development of a
multi-start root-finding scheme and its ancillary algebraic identities.

\subsection*{Future directions}

Natural extensions are: non-cyclotomic anchor families (general
polynomials), which would require dropping the closed-form cyclotomic
identities that drive Sections~\ref{sec:cyclotomic} and~\ref{sec:zeta};
and a formalization of Fatou--Julia in Mathlib, which would make a
proper in-Lean proof of McMullen's 1987 theorem accessible. We make
no conjecture regarding the other three problems discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:open-problems}.
